On a summer Sunday, Sixth Avenue becomes something more than a busy Manhattan thoroughfare. Dominican flags fill the street, music carries between the buildings, and families and neighbors gather to celebrate a heritage that is also an essential part of New York City's identity.

That was the spirit of the **44th National Dominican Day Parade**, held on August 9, 2026, under the theme *Dominicanos: Identidad y Cultura*. The parade brought thousands of people to Midtown for an afternoon of music, dance, colorful costumes and community pride [1].

At **ARAC International**, we support the Dominican Day Parade and cultural celebrations like it for a straightforward reason: **they are Positive Peace in action.** They give communities a visible place in the city, invite neighbors to learn from one another, and remind us that cultural identity is something to celebrate, not a barrier to living together.

## A celebration that belongs to the whole city

The joy of a parade is immediate. You hear the music before you see the procession. You recognize the flags, the dancing and the pride on people's faces. Parents bring children, friends gather along the route, and traditions are shared with a new generation.

But there is another kind of value in these moments. New Yorkers who may not share a language, birthplace or family history can still share the same sidewalk, enjoy the same performance and come away knowing a little more about one another.

That is how **social cohesion** grows: not only through formal meetings or public statements, but through ordinary encounters that make our neighbors more familiar.

## Culture is a form of public expression

The Dominican Day Parade gives participants an opportunity to tell their own story. Music, clothing, dance and the Dominican flag communicate a history and a sense of belonging that a speech alone cannot capture.

The Institute for Economics & Peace identifies **Free Flow of Information** and **Acceptance of the Rights of Others** among the eight Pillars of Positive Peace [2]. Both are visible in the spirit of these celebrations.

Free expression includes the ability of communities to share their experiences and heritage openly. Acceptance means making room for different identities and traditions within the same city. We do not need to be Dominican to appreciate what Dominican culture contributes to New York.

{% include video.html n=3 %}

These gatherings also serve as an invitation. When a community brings its traditions into public space, everyone has an opportunity to observe, ask questions, listen and learn. That is a simple but valuable form of cultural exchange.

## Good governance is something people can experience

A celebration of this scale does not happen without coordination. Organizers, volunteers, public agencies and city services help make a busy avenue available for a major community event. Ahead of the 2026 parade, city officials publicly described preparations with organizers and community groups, including street closures and safety arrangements [3].

This is one practical way to understand the Positive Peace pillar of **Well-Functioning Government**.

Good governance is not limited to elections, legislation or municipal offices. Residents also experience it when public institutions help make civic spaces accessible, support safe participation and recognize the communities that give the city its character.

{% include video.html n=4 %}

Of course, a parade by itself does not establish how well a government performs in every area. It does show something worth recognizing: public institutions and community organizations working together to make cultural participation possible.

## Why ARAC supports parades like this one

The Dominican Day Parade is one expression of a much larger New York story. The Puerto Rican Day Parade and other heritage celebrations across the five boroughs bring communities into view, preserve traditions and encourage people from different backgrounds to meet in shared public spaces.

At ARAC, we believe those opportunities matter. Positive Peace is built through the attitudes, institutions and relationships that help people live well together. Celebrations cannot solve every challenge facing a community, nor should they be expected to. But they can strengthen belonging, mutual respect and the everyday connections on which a resilient city depends.

The lasting value of the National Dominican Day Parade is therefore larger than a day's festivities. It is found in the children learning their heritage, the neighbors discovering something new, and a city making room for its communities to be seen and heard.

**We support these celebrations because a city that makes space for its many cultures also makes space for stronger relationships between its people.**
