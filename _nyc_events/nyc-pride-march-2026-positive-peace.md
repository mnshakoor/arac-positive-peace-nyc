On Sunday, June 28, 2026, New York City filled Fifth Avenue for the annual NYC Pride March. The march started at noon at 26th Street, moved past the historic Stonewall Inn, and finished near 15th Street and Seventh Avenue. We were there to document it, and the footage in this article shows a city that brings many cultures, ages and backgrounds together in one public space.

## "For All of Us"

This year's theme was "For All of Us," inspired by a quotation attributed to Marsha P. Johnson, a Stonewall veteran and activist:

> "There is no pride for some of us without liberation for all of us."

The idea is simple and strong. A community is only truly proud when everyone in it is included, and that includes people who are often left out of the conversation first. It is a message that fits well with the work we do at ARAC International.

## What is Positive Peace?

Positive Peace is a concept developed by the Institute for Economics and Peace (IEP). It describes [the attitudes, institutions and structures that create and sustain peaceful societies](https://www.visionofhumanity.org/the-eight-pillars-of-positive-peace/). Peace, in this view, is more than the absence of conflict. It is the presence of the conditions that let people live safely, participate fully and trust one another.

IEP organizes these conditions into eight pillars. Several of them show up naturally at a gathering like Pride.

## Diversity as a source of peace

**Acceptance of the Rights of Others.** This pillar is about whether people of every identity can live openly and take part in public life. Pride is one of the clearest everyday expressions of it. A march that welcomes people of every gender, race, culture and faith tells everyone watching that they belong.

**Good Relations with Neighbors.** A large march moves through many neighborhoods and draws residents, families, local businesses and visitors into the same shared moment. Events like this build familiarity and trust across groups, which is how communities handle disagreement without conflict.

**Free Flow of Information.** When people can gather, speak and share what they see, communities stay informed and connected. Community media, photography and video, including the clips on this page, help carry those stories beyond the avenue itself.

## A city that shows its many cultures

New York is one of the most culturally diverse cities in the world, and Pride puts that diversity on display. Different languages, traditions, styles and histories share the same route. That visibility matters. When people see themselves and their neighbors represented in public, the sense of belonging grows, and belonging is a foundation of lasting peace.

## Building Positive Peace in your own community

Positive Peace is not only built by governments and institutions. It grows through everyday choices: showing up for neighbors, welcoming people who are different from us, and telling honest stories about the communities we share. Events like the NYC Pride March are a reminder that inclusion is something people practice together, in public, year after year.

At ARAC Community, we will keep documenting the moments where culture, inclusion and peace meet. Share this article with someone in your community, and tell us how diversity strengthens the place where you live.
